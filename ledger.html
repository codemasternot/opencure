On-chain proof. Off-chain medicine.

Hashes can help verify that an approved artifact has not changed without publishing the underlying patient-linked file.

Privacy boundary

Patient identity is never written to the public ledger.

A participating institution can derive a pseudonymous case reference using a private identifier and an institution-held secret. Sensitive source files remain encrypted in controlled storage.

patientHash = SHA256(patientPrivateId + hospitalSecretSalt)
reportHash = SHA256(finalResearchReport)
vaccineFileHash = SHA256(encryptedVaccineDesignFile)
  • ✓Public hashes are not substitutes for access control.
  • ✓Source files remain encrypted and off-chain.
  • ✓Authorized parties can verify artifact integrity.

What belongs where.

The architecture is intentionally conservative about what becomes public.

Off-chain

Protected research and medical data

Patient identity, raw genomic data, medical records, consent records, sensitive research files, access-control metadata, and other protected information remain in controlled systems.

On-chain / public verification

Minimal proofs and funding references

Non-identifying artifact hashes, approved transaction references, grant movements, and milestone identifiers can provide auditability without publishing the underlying records.

Security assumptions must be explicit.

A hash does not magically anonymize weak identifiers. The design depends on strong secret handling, access controls, appropriate salts or keyed constructions, and institution-level privacy processes.

Strong identifiersAvoid guessable patient references and predictable inputs.
Secret managementInstitution-held secrets must be stored and rotated securely.
Encrypted storageSensitive source artifacts remain encrypted outside public infrastructure.
Least disclosurePublish only what is necessary for verification or transparency.
