RNA vaccine research

From molecular signal to a testable candidate hypothesis.

The OpenCure workflow is built to reduce wasted effort on weak leads. It does not treat model output as clinical evidence; candidates remain research hypotheses until independently validated.

The research pipeline.

Each stage is designed to leave an auditable trail of inputs, assumptions, model outputs, disagreement, and validation requirements.

Four-stage workflow

Generate less noise. Preserve more evidence.

The system is meant to help scientific teams focus scarce sequencing, synthesis, assay, and expert-review resources on better-supported candidates.

1
Ingest de-identified molecular dataPrepare variant calls, expression context, HLA or relevant immunogenetic context, and reference data under a controlled data-governance process.
2
Generate candidate hypothesesUse specialist systems to propose target sets and RNA design hypotheses under explicit design constraints.
3
Score across independent modelsCompare binding, expression, structure-aware signals, immunogenicity estimates, safety filters, uncertainty, and disagreement.
4
Prepare validation packagesExport reproducible reports with model provenance, assumptions, failure modes, and a proposed experimental validation path.

Validation gates matter more than model confidence.

A high model score is not evidence that a therapy works. OpenCure separates computational prioritization from experimental and clinical validation.

Gate 01

Computational review

Check data quality, replicate model runs, inspect disagreement, verify provenance, and challenge assumptions before any physical work begins.

Gate 02

Wet-lab validation

Qualified partners determine appropriate assays, controls, quality checks, and experimental endpoints before drawing translational conclusions.

Gate 03

Regulated translation

Clinical, veterinary, ethics, and regulatory decisions remain with appropriately qualified and authorized institutions.

What a research evidence package should contain.

The aim is to make a result reviewable by someone who did not run the original analysis.

Input provenanceDataset origin, preprocessing steps, exclusions, and versioned references.
Model provenanceModel identity, version, parameters, and relevant model limitations.
Uncertainty reportConsensus, disagreement, confidence indicators, and unresolved questions.
Validation planProposed assays, controls, success criteria, and explicit failure criteria.
